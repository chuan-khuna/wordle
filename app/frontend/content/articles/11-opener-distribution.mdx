The <Term>Benchmark</Term> played one <Term>Opener</Term> for each <Term>Strategy</Term>. But the Solver can start with any of the 14,855 words on the <Term>Guess List</Term>. So let us play them all.

For each Strategy, every word is the Opener of 2,315 Games, one for each Answer, and the Solver finishes each Game on its own. That is 14,855 × 2,315 Games, and we keep one number for each: the <Term>Guess Count</Term>. The table of all of them is the <Term>Guess Count Matrix</Term>. Average each row and you have the <Term>Average Guess Count</Term> of every Opener: how many Guesses it needs, on average, to win.

Now count how many Openers have each Average Guess Count. This is the <Term>Opener Distribution</Term>. Each block is one Opener, and they are stacked in bars 0.01 wide, the best at the bottom. The Solver here uses Expected Information over all words. All 14,855 words are drawn: one colour for the words on the <Term>Answer List</Term>, another for the rest. Four words are marked, and each has a card with its numbers. Click a bar to list the Openers in it:

## What it shows

The best Opener is <Word>tarse</Word>, with an Average Guess Count of **3.429**. The worst is <Word>qajaq</Word>, with **4.117**. That is less than one Guess between the best start and the worst. Choosing the Opener matters, but not nearly as much as playing the rest of the Game well.

<Word>tarse</Word> is not an Answer, and neither is <Word>salet</Word>. They are on the <Term>Guess List</Term>, so both are in the chart, and <Word>tarse</Word> is the best block of all. On the full page you can turn on Answer List only to see just the words that can be the answer.

<Word>crane</Word> comes seventh, with 3.437, less than a hundredth of a Guess behind <Word>tarse</Word>. <Word>raise</Word> is 95th, with 3.471. Both are good Openers, and the gap between them is about three hundredths of a Guess.

The Strategy matters. With Expected Information over Candidates only, the best Opener is <Word>salet</Word> with **3.528**, and <Word>raise</Word> falls to 420th. The Opener that is best depends on how the Solver plays the rest of the Game.

## Two rankings that disagree

Article 7 ranked the Openers by Expected Information, before any Game was played. Playing them out gives a second ranking, and the two do not agree.

<Word>raise</Word> is fifth by Expected Information, with 5.878 bits, but 95th when played. <Word>crane</Word> is only 35th by Expected Information, with 5.743 bits, and seventh when played. Only <Word>tarse</Word> is first in both.

Expected Information measures one step: how much the first Guess tells you. A Game is several steps. A high score on the first step does not promise a good finish.

## Popular Openers, played out

Some Openers are famous. Here is how they fare under Expected Information over all words.

The vowel-heavy starts are the weakest. <Word>adieu</Word> is 7,062nd, with 3.624, so about half the Guess List opens better. <Word>audio</Word> is 8,241st, with 3.644, and <Word>ouija</Word> is near the bottom, 14,197th, with 3.822. Four vowels tell you a lot about vowels and little about anything else.

The Expected Information favourites are good but not the best. <Word>soare</Word> is third by Expected Information and 71st when played, and <Word>roate</Word> is fourth and 58th.

The common-letter words live up to their name. <Word>crate</Word> is 4th, <Word>trace</Word> 5th, <Word>slate</Word> 6th and <Word>crane</Word> 7th.

At the bottom are rare letters and repeats: <Word>qajaq</Word>, <Word>xviii</Word>, <Word>zhuzh</Word>. Among the Answers, <Word>jazzy</Word> is the worst, with 4.029. Even these never lose a Game.

### salet or slate?

Under this Strategy <Word>salet</Word> is 3rd, with 3.432, and <Word>slate</Word> 6th, with 3.437. Over 2,315 Games that is 7,944 Guesses against 7,957, a difference of 13. With Expected Information over Candidates only, they are first and second, one Guess apart. Under the two Strategies that score by Expected Candidates, <Word>slate</Word> comes out ahead.

So it is a tie. <Word>slate</Word> has one advantage: it is an Answer, so once in 2,315 Games it wins in one Guess. <Word>salet</Word> never can.
