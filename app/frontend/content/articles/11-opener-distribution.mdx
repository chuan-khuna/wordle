The <Term>Benchmark</Term> played one <Term>Opener</Term> for each <Term>Strategy</Term>. But the Solver can start with any of the 14,855 words on the <Term>Guess List</Term>. This Article plays all of them.

For each Strategy, each word in its <Term>Guess Pool</Term> is the Opener of 2,315 Games, one Game for each Answer. The Solver plays each Game to the end. For the Guess List, that is 14,855 × 2,315 Games. For each Game, we keep one number, the <Term>Guess Count</Term>. The table of all these numbers is the <Term>Guess Count Matrix</Term>. The mean of one row is the <Term>Average Guess Count</Term> of one Opener: the number of Guesses that it needs to win, on average.

Then count how many Openers have each Average Guess Count. This is the <Term>Opener Distribution</Term>. In the chart, each block is one Opener. The blocks are in bars 0.01 wide, with the best Opener at the bottom. The Strategy is Expected Information, Guess List, All words. The chart shows all 14,855 words, with one colour for the words on the <Term>Answer List</Term> and a different colour for the other words. The tabs below the chart mark lists of words (the best Openers, some myths and some bad Openers), and each marked word shows its numbers above it. Click a bar to see the Openers in it:

## What it shows

The best Opener is <Word>tarse</Word>, with an Average Guess Count of **3.429**. The worst is <Word>qajaq</Word>, with **4.117**. The best start and the worst start are less than one Guess apart. The Opener is important, but it is much less important than how the Solver plays the rest of the Game.

<Word>tarse</Word> is not an Answer, and <Word>salet</Word> is not an Answer either. Both are on the <Term>Guess List</Term>, so the chart shows both, and <Word>tarse</Word> is the best block of all. On the full page, select Only Openers on the Answer List to see only the words that can be the Answer.

<Word>crane</Word> is seventh, with 3.437, less than a hundredth of a Guess behind <Word>tarse</Word>. <Word>raise</Word> is 95th, with 3.471. Both are good Openers, and the difference between them is about three hundredths of a Guess.

The Strategy is important. With Expected Information, Guess List, Candidates only, the best Opener is <Word>salet</Word> with **3.528**, and <Word>raise</Word> goes down to 420th. The best Opener depends on how the Solver plays the rest of the Game.

## Two rankings that disagree

Article 7 ranked the Openers by Expected Information, before the Solver played any Game. When the Solver plays each Opener to the end, we get a second ranking. The two rankings do not agree.

<Word>raise</Word> is fifth by Expected Information, with 5.878 bits, but 95th when the Solver plays it. <Word>crane</Word> is only 35th by Expected Information, with 5.743 bits, but seventh when the Solver plays it. Only <Word>tarse</Word> is first in both rankings.

Expected Information measures one step: how much the first Guess tells you. A Game has several steps. A good first step does not make sure of a good result.

## Popular Openers, played out

Some Openers are famous. This is how they do under Expected Information, Guess List, All words.

The Openers with many vowels are the weakest. <Word>adieu</Word> is 7,062nd, with 3.624, so about half of the Guess List is a better start. <Word>audio</Word> is 8,241st, with 3.644, and <Word>ouija</Word> is near the bottom, 14,197th, with 3.822. Four vowels tell you a lot about the vowels and little about the other letters.

The favourites of Expected Information are good, but not the best. <Word>soare</Word> is third by Expected Information and 71st when played. <Word>roate</Word> is fourth and 58th.

Words with common letters do well. <Word>crate</Word> is 4th, <Word>trace</Word> 5th, <Word>slate</Word> 6th and <Word>crane</Word> 7th.

At the bottom are words with rare letters and repeated letters: <Word>qajaq</Word>, <Word>xviii</Word> and <Word>zhuzh</Word>. Of the Answers, <Word>jazzy</Word> is the worst, with 4.029. But even these words never lose a Game.

### salet or slate?

Under this Strategy, <Word>salet</Word> is 3rd, with 3.432, and <Word>slate</Word> is 6th, with 3.437. Over 2,315 Games, that is 7,944 Guesses against 7,957, a difference of 13. With the Guess List and Candidates only, they are first and second, one Guess apart. With the two Guess List Strategies that rank by Expected Candidates, <Word>slate</Word> is better.

So the two words are equal in practice. <Word>slate</Word> has one advantage. It is an Answer, so in one Game out of 2,315 it wins with the first Guess. <Word>salet</Word> can never do that.
