We can now build the <Term>Solver</Term>. Its <Term>Strategy</Term> is the simplest one there is: at every step, look at the current <Term>Candidate Set</Term>, rate every Guess by its <Term>Expected Information</Term>, and play the best. This is called greedy, because it takes the best Guess for now and never plans further ahead.

Each step is the three ideas of this Part in a row. Find the Candidates. Split them with every Guess. Play the top one. That Guess is the <Term>Suggestion</Term>.

```python title="The greedy choice" {11-14}
def suggest(
    self,
    candidates: np.ndarray,
    *,
    pool: Pool = "guesses",
    scope: Scope = "global",
    by: Rank = "bits",
) -> WordStats | None:
    """The Suggestion: the top-ranked Guess, or None if no Candidate is left."""
    # With one or two Candidates, guessing one of them is never worse.
    if 0 < len(candidates) <= 2:
        scope = "candidates"
    best = self.rank(candidates, pool=pool, scope=scope, by=by, top=1)
    return best[0] if best else None
```

The loop around it is short. Guess the Suggestion, narrow the Candidates with the Feedback, and repeat:

```python
game = Game("watch")
candidates = solver.all_candidates()
while not game.is_over:
    guess = game.guess(solver.suggest(candidates).word)
    candidates = solver.narrow(candidates, guess.word, pack(guess.feedback))
```

## A Guess that can't win

One setting in `suggest` decides a lot: `scope`, the <Term>Search Scope</Term>. The Solver may search only the Candidates, or it may search **All words** on the Guess List, including words that cannot be the Answer.

Playing a word that cannot win sounds wasteful. But it can be the best question. Here is why, in a Game where the Answer is <Word>watch</Word>.

First, the Solver uses the narrow setting, Candidates only, and takes its Guesses from the Answer List, its <Term>Guess Pool</Term>. Step through it:

<StepThroughGame id="trap" answer="watch" pool="answers" scope="candidates" step={4} />

<Word>raise</Word>, <Word>tangy</Word> and <Word>caput</Word> bring the Candidate Set down to 5 words: <Word>batch</Word>, <Word>hatch</Word>, <Word>latch</Word>, <Word>match</Word> and <Word>watch</Word>. Every word fits <Word>_atch</Word>. Now the Solver has to pick one of them, and each is a poor question. Guess <Word>batch</Word> and, unless it is right, you only learn the first letter was wrong. The best a Candidate can do is 0.72 bits, with a largest group of 4. The Solver works through <Word>batch</Word>, <Word>hatch</Word>, <Word>latch</Word> and <Word>match</Word> one at a time. <Word>watch</Word> takes **eight** Guesses, and under the Guess Limit of six that is a <Term>Loss</Term>. <Word>match</Word>, with seven, is a Loss too.

This is the <Word>_atch</Word> trap. A family of words that differ in a single letter is a trap for any Strategy that only guesses Candidates, because every Candidate rules out only itself.

Now the same Answer, with the Solver allowed to search All words on the Guess List:

This time the Solver opens with <Word>tarse</Word> and, from 5 Candidates, plays <Word>abamp</Word>. It is a strange word, and it cannot be the Answer. But it holds <Letter>b</Letter>, <Letter>m</Letter> and <Letter>a</Letter> together, and it asks four questions at once. Its Feedback separates the family, so the Solver wins in 5 Guesses. <MarginNote>Follow <Word>watch</Word> with this Strategy on the [Follow an Answer page](/follow?answer=watch&pool=guesses&scope=global&by=bits), and see the Candidates left after every Guess.</MarginNote>

You can see the same thing with the same 5 Candidates. A Candidate such as <Word>batch</Word> is worth 0.72 bits, with 3.4 words left on average. The best word on the whole list, <Word>ablow</Word>, is worth 1.92 bits and leaves 1.4 words on average, with no group larger than 2. It cannot win now, and it nearly guarantees a win next.

The rule of thumb: **a Guess that cannot win can still be the best Guess.** Searching every word is what turns eight Guesses into five. The last line of `suggest` has one more detail. With two Candidates left it goes back to Candidates only, because then guessing one is never worse.
